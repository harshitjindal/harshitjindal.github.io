Question 1.1

Customer behavior in the product market is constantly changing. Give two or more examples of how software is used in the development of non-IT products. How does product development respond to or align with the customer’s ongoing behavior? Can you give an example? – 3%

There is no doubt that customer behaviour in the product market is constantly changing, which is why it is important to focus more on the outcomes, instead of the outputs. Outcomes are measurable changes in customer behaviour, and having outcome driven teams are important to achieve organizational agility.

A couple of examples of how software is used in the development of non-IT products are:

  • Finance – Credit card companies use sophisticated machine learning models for credit and fraud risk modeling. Similarly, High-Frequency Trading (HFT) companies employ advanced computer algorithms at the heart of their business that make decisions in sub-millisecond latency.
  • Automotive Industry – Tesla is a great example of the use of software in automotive sector. The self-driving capabilities, the range detection feature, as well as the infotainment system rely on software to power them. Mercedes Benz uses a subscription based model powered by software that enables their consumers to unlock additional capabilities in their vehicles, for example, the Mercedes EQS series allows a consumer to pay $60 per month to unlock an addition 60 horsepower. 1
  • Defence – Advanced defence equipment is highly likely to be relying on software to power it. For example, Israel’s Iron Dome defence system uses software to analyze incoming projectile’s path, and launches countermeasures accordingly.
  • Journalism – As in the HBR webinar, journalism houses like Bloomberg identify themselves as software-first companies, and their audience consumes their content via digital means powered by software.
  • Electronics – IOT devices like smart lights, smart speakers all rely on software to make them smart.

Product development teams sense and respond to customer’s ongoing behaviour in a variety of ways, where they use their best-guess to improve the outcome, and take steps towards it. They then analyze the customer behaviour and choose to make further modifications / refinements, or to rollback the change. For example, Facebook’s design team was sensing which shade of blue was received most favorably by its users, and they responded by choosing to keep the same shade that was the most widely liked. Another example could be Amazon deploying its code to production, to real users, every 11.6 seconds, making adjustments basis the customer behavioural patterns. 2

Question 1.2

What aspects of product development must be considered when an organization transitions from being agile to doing agile and to attain organizational agility? Explain each of these features. – 2%

The original intention of the agile manifesto was to be agile, where the focus was to learn faster, get customer feedback sooner, deliver value sooner, and to ensure we’re building the right thing. Over the years, the reasons of adopting agile have changed, and we are transitioning from being agile to doing agile by shifting the focus to ship features faster and more predictably.

Organization agility is the ability of an enterprise to maneuver quickly, allowing it to respond to change faster and gain competitve edge.

The following aspects of product development must be kept in mind when an organization is transitioning from being agile to doing agile, and to focus on attaining organizational agility are:

  • People – There is a need to hire and retain the right teams which are focussed on learning and are outcome driven. The colleagues in a team should be independent, collaborative, humble and transparent for them to be able to work in an agile way.
  • Funding model – Evidence based funding can significantly improve the efficacy with which the limited finances of an organization are utilized to generate value. The timeframes between different funding cycles should be shorter, and the evidence of acheived outcomes of each team should be kept in mind while budgeting further money.
  • Incentives – The teams should have a safe space to be wrong, and the focus should be on learning quicker and being self-organizing. They need to be encouraged to focus on the generated positive impact or the outcomes, which are measurable changes to customer behaviour, and they should be incentivized to be transparent, collaborative and have open communication.
  • Authority – If the measures of success are objectively defined, and the decision making is brought closed to the lower levels of an organization which is closest to the product, it results in empowered and autonomous teams which should be encouraged to be cross-functional, and data-informed instead of being data-driven.

Question 1.3

Describe the switch from the conventional to the agile recruitment model. What are the attributes of the applicants to be investigated? – 2%

In order to attract and retain the people that are capable of driving organizational agility, there needs to be a switch from the conventional to the agile recruitment model. This can be done by externalization of the work we’re doing, and by being transparent and showing people what sort of emphasis the organization has on learning.

The traditional recruitment model incentivises time-to-fill, and focuses on checking off boxes of skill sets which in turn creates a barrier between different disciplines. This is bound to minimize cooperation between different teams, whereas with the agile recruitment model, what should be done instead is to hire people who have an entrepreneurial mindset, the multi-faceted thinkers who don’t neatly fit inside isolated boxes of skill sets. The focus should be on the retention rate over the time-to-fill rate, which in turn reflects throughout the organization as higher team moralle.

A couple of attributes of the application to be investigated are:

  • How they collaborate with the target teams – If the potential candidates are given a chance to engage with the target teams, we can learn if they are a good fit, if they are collaborative, humble, and flexible with the teams they are going to be working with. This gives a great opportunity to understand if the team together will be greater than the sum of its individual members.
  • How they interact with people who are not their peers – How an applicant behaves with someone they don’t work with is a great indicator of their character and what sort of culture they will bring to the team. For example, a person rude to the cleaning staff is unlikely to be humble, and will hesitate in admitting mistakes instead of learning and growing.

Further, just like agile retrospectives, there is a need to reflect on efficacy of techniques followed by a recruitment team where there is an opportunity to identify potential challenge areas, learn and improve upon them to ensure organizational success.

Question 1.4

You’re working on a computer diagnostic app. You’re assigning output-based and outcome-based goals to your team? What are these goals, and how do you set them? How do you intend to finance this team? – 3%

Output-based goals are the ones which, by nature, are the steps and processes an organization or company implements to achieve a goal or milestone.3 For example, “develop this iOS app” is one such goal, where the possibilities are if the app has been developed or not. Outcome-based goals are focussed on the measurable changes to customer behaviour, and can be defined as the results that an organization wants. In other words, outputs can be thought of as actions, and are easy to validate, whereas outcomes are the impact achieved which can also be qualitative, and hence harder to evaluate.

For a computer diagnostic app, the following could be some output-based and outcome-based goals:

  • Output-based goals:
    • Use “Flutter” as a modern cross-platform framework to build the app
    • Identify the 15 most common issues, and enable diagnostics for those
    • Identify and add a paywall for the 5 most premium / advanced issues that the app can diagnose
  • Outcome-based goals
    • App should be intuitive to use
    • Generate $5 million revenue in the current year
    • There should be a 20% increase in active user-base
    • Customer feedback ratings should be higher than 4.5 out of 5
    • App should be rated amonst the top 5 within the product segment
    • User should be able to achieve their task in the least amount of effort

The way these goals have been set is to plan output-goals such that they drive the outcome-goals. For example, using Flutter as a cross-platform framework to build the app would allow the team to maintain a single codebase while being able to cater to Android, iOS, as well as the webusers, which will in turn help drive the outcome-goal of increasing active user-base by 20%.

The way I would finance this team is to have shorter review cycles, say quarterly, and focus on the the outcome-based goals set for the team against the evidence of progressing towards these goals since the last review cycle. I would adjust the output-goals where required, based on the learnings from the past reviews. This would encourage the team to focus on the outcomes, instead of just the outputs, and consequently drive value more efficiently.
